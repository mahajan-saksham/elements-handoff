## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

This is the canonical reference for the **Elements by Vaidyam** brand. It is intended to live inside a Claude Project as knowledge.

**Claude, when this document is in your project knowledge, behave as follows:**

1. **Treat this document as the source of truth.** Brand decisions captured here — positioning, voice, colour, type, the Pancha system — are settled. Don't relitigate them unless the founder explicitly asks to revisit.
2. **Be a senior design + brand strategy collaborator, not a generic assistant.** The founder is the decision-maker; you are the partner who builds, critiques, and proposes. Argue with evidence. Push back when something contradicts the system below.
3. **Default to specifics, not abstractions.** "Use brand colours" is useless. "Background `#F7F2E6`, italic accent in `#C4541E` Cormorant Garamond" is useful.
4. **Show, then explain.** When proposing UI, mockups, copy, or layout — render the visual first (via the visualize tool when available), then explain the thinking. Never the other way around.
5. **The Pancha system is structural, not decorative.** Every visual, every product page, every email, every campaign must respect the five-element architecture. If something can't be mapped to an element or doesn't need to be, leave the system alone — never invent a sixth element.
6. **Hold the visual language strictly.** Direction 3 (Warm Editorial) is the master visual system. Directions 1 and 2 exist as reserved variants for specific surfaces (manifesto, product pages). Spec violations are not "creative interpretations" — they're errors.
7. **Bias toward Indian context without being orientalist.** The brand is built for urban Indian millennials and Gen Z. References should default to Indian cities, Indian creators, Indian use cases. But never lean on "ancient wisdom" mysticism — it's banned.
8. **Quick commerce is always in the room.** Every design, every campaign, every product decision should be evaluated for how it performs on a Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart thumbnail at 64×64 px.
9. **Citations are non-negotiable.** Every clinical claim gets a study citation in IBM Plex Mono caption style. No exceptions.
10. **Founder's working style:** prefers comprehensive answers built progressively, not high-level summaries. Doesn't mind length when the content earns it. Wants options with sharp differentiation, not safe variants of the same idea. Pushes back when things feel undercooked — when this happens, redo with more craft, don't defend.

# PART 1 · WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

## 1.1 The brand at a glance

**Name:** Elements by Vaidyam
**Category:** Clinical Ayurveda — premium-accessible nutraceuticals and adaptogenic ingestibles
**Market:** Indian metros + Tier 1–2 cities (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Chandigarh)
**Distribution:** Quick commerce primary (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart, Swiggy IM, BB Now). D2C site and Amazon/Nykaa secondary.
**Stage:** Pre-launch (as of May 2026)
**Price band:** ₹150–₹1,500 per SKU. Premium-accessible. Above Dabur/Himalaya, below Forest Essentials.
**Hero SKU:** Adaptogenic Instant Coffee — KSM-66 ashwagandha + L-theanine in a single-serve sachet
**Supplement line:** Elements Five — Brahmi Rasayana, Pure Shilajit Resin, Herbal Supplement, Triphala Churna, Ashwagandha Tablets

## 1.2 The conceptual model

Elements by Vaidyam is built on three structural ideas working together:

**(a) Clinical Ayurveda as positioning.** Indian Ayurveda has two failure modes consumers reject: the Patanjali-coded mass-market aesthetic (saffron, Devanagari, godman politics) and the unbranded Amazon-generic capsule (no extract standardisation, no clinical references). The market gap is *premium, science-validated, transparent* Ayurveda — what Mintel India 2025 explicitly called "ancient ingredients, modern format, science-respected." Elements occupies this gap by leading with KSM-66 (Ixoreal, root-only, FSSAI-compliant), publishing withanolide percentages on pack, citing peer-reviewed studies, and pairing every product with a real named in-house Vaidya (Ayurvedic doctor, BAMS minimum).

**(b) The Pancha Mahabhuta as architecture.** The brand's name is *elements*. The five classical Ayurvedic elements — Akasha (ether), Vayu (air), Agni (fire), Jala (water), Prithvi (earth) — become the organising system for every product, every concern, every visual, every campaign. Each product carries one or two element tags. The quiz returns an elemental profile. The AI vaidya speaks in elemental terms. This is what makes the brand structurally distinctive and impossible to copy without being obvious about it.

**(c) Quiz-first discovery + AI Vaidya + ecom.** The default consumer journey is: land on the homepage → take the 2-minute Pancha quiz → receive an elemental profile (bar chart of your five-element composition) → meet the AI Vaidya (an LLM grounded in classical Ayurvedic texts + the brand's clinical content) → receive a personalised 14-day ritual recommendation → purchase. The AI Vaidya is the loyalty mechanic; the ritual is the retention mechanic; the ecom is the conversion mechanic.

# PART 2 · STRATEGIC FOUNDATION

## 2.1 Master positioning

> **Clinical Ayurveda.**
> Ancient ingredients, clinical proofs. The first Indian Ayurveda brand that earns trust the way modern medicine does — named extracts, dose disclosure, peer-reviewed studies, a real named vaidya.

This positioning is structurally defensible because most competitors physically cannot move here without reformulating their products and rebuilding their supply chain. It addresses the credibility gap that Mintel India 2025, HerbVerdict 2026, and consumer voice on Quora/Reddit all flag in the category. It's the gap no one in Indian premium-accessible Ayurveda occupies.

## 2.2 The "why now" — market context

- **India nutraceuticals market:** USD 32.14 Bn (2024), projected USD 75.81 Bn by 2033 at 10% CAGR (Grand View Research)
- **Average age of supplementation entry dropped from 40+ to 24** (Deeksha Rajani, founder of Be, in Entrepreneur India)
- **62% of Indian employees report work-related burnout — triple the global average of 20%** (CII-MediBuddy July 2024)
- **Q-commerce GMV trajectory:** USD 500 Mn (FY22) → USD 3.34 Bn (FY24) → USD 9.95 Bn projected by 2029
- **AOV on Blinkit ~₹709, Instamart ~₹619** projected for 2026; wellness is the explicit "next leg of GMV growth"
- **Recent M&A in the space:** HUL acquired remaining 49% of OZiva for ₹824 Cr (Feb 2026 — ₹1,682 Cr valuation). USV acquired 79% of Wellbeing Nutrition (Feb 2026 — ₹1,583 Cr valuation). Estée Lauder announced acquisition of remaining 51% of Forest Essentials (March 5 2026). Marico has Plix Life. ITC has Yoga Bar.
- **FSSAI advisory April 16, 2026:** Only ashwagandha **roots and root extracts** are permitted in food supplements. Leaves are not permitted. KSM-66 is root-only by design — Elements is on the right side of this regardless of how the litigation (currently stayed in Karnataka HC) resolves.

The category is consolidating into FMCG-owned brands. Pre-launch brands have a 24–36 month window to establish defensible differentiation before facing either acquisition or competition from those acquirers' scale. Elements' moat is positioning + clinical evidence + brand world — not scale.

## 2.3 The three positioning territories (and how they map)

We considered five territories; three are active:

| Territory | Use | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Clinical Ayurveda** | Master positioning across all surfaces | **Primary** |
| **Calm is the new caffeine** | Hero coffee SKU campaign + Instagram | **Tactical (coffee)** |
| **The Modern Apothecary** | Visual aesthetic + lifestyle umbrella | **Visual umbrella** |

The first is what we *say* the brand is. The second is what we *say* about the hero SKU. The third is what we *look and feel like*.

## 2.4 The opportunity gap we own

There is no Indian Ayurveda brand that simultaneously:
- Looks like a modern apothecary (frosted glass, lowercase serif, restrained palette)
- Reads like a clinical document (named extracts, withanolide %, peer-reviewed citations, batch numbers)
- Tastes/feels like a daily ritual the consumer enjoys
- Costs less than imported AG1 (₹1,500 ceiling)
- Lives natively on Blinkit's wellness aisle with sub-10-minute delivery

That gap is Elements' territory.

# PART 6 · THE PANCHA SYSTEM

## 6.1 The five elements

The classical Pancha Mahabhuta — five elements that compose all matter and experience in Ayurvedic philosophy. We use them as the brand's organising architecture, not as decoration.

### Akasha (Ether / Space)
- **Sanskrit code:** AK
- **Brand colour:** `#5B4E7A` (deep muted indigo-violet)
- **Wash:** `#DCD2EA`
- **Domain:** Consciousness, clarity, openness, mental spaciousness, the mind
- **Concerns it addresses:** Brain fog, mental loops, racing thoughts, lack of clarity, decision fatigue
- **Products tagged Akasha:** Brahmi Rasayana (primary), occasionally featured in Coffee

### Vayu (Air)
- **Sanskrit code:** VA
- **Brand colour:** `#6A8AA8` (soft slate blue)
- **Wash:** `#D2E2E8`
- **Domain:** Movement, focus, breath, the nervous system, attention
- **Concerns it addresses:** Distraction, jitter, anxious energy, scattered focus, restlessness
- **Products tagged Vayu:** Coffee (secondary tag), Brahmi (secondary)

### Agni (Fire)
- **Sanskrit code:** AG
- **Brand colour:** `#C4541E` (warm sienna / burnt terracotta)
- **Wash:** `#F5D3A8`
- **Domain:** Transformation, energy, digestion, metabolism, vitality
- **Concerns it addresses:** Low energy, sluggish digestion, afternoon crash, low motivation
- **Products tagged Agni:** Coffee (primary), Shilajit (primary), Triphala (secondary)

### Jala (Water)
- **Sanskrit code:** JA
- **Brand colour:** `#2E7A75` (deep muted teal)
- **Wash:** `#C7DDDA`
- **Domain:** Calm, flow, restoration, hydration, emotional regulation
- **Concerns it addresses:** Stress, emotional volatility, dryness, lack of recovery, agitation
- **Products tagged Jala:** Ashwagandha (secondary)

### Prithvi (Earth)
- **Sanskrit code:** PR
- **Brand colour:** `#6B7A3D` (olive sage)
- **Wash:** `#DCE3CE`
- **Domain:** Ground, strength, sleep, stability, structure
- **Concerns it addresses:** Insomnia, anxiety, weakness, lack of stamina, ungroundedness
- **Products tagged Prithvi:** Ashwagandha (primary), Triphala (primary), Shilajit (secondary)

## 6.2 How elements show up across the brand

**On product:** Each pack carries 1–2 element tags as small dot+code badges (e.g., `● AG · VA`).
**In the quiz:** 7 questions return a percentage breakdown across all 5 elements. The user's "elemental profile" is the bar chart.
**In recommendations:** The AI Vaidya recommends products that *balance* the elements you're running high or low on.
**In typography:** The italic element name (e.g., *vayu*) appears next to every concern label in body copy and CTAs.
**In iconography:** Each element has a minimal abstract symbol (see iconography section). Used sparingly — element dots and italic names do most of the work.
**In editorial calendar:** Each month can be assigned an element ("The Vayu Issue" of the Elements Letter). Quarterly campaigns rotate through pairings.

## 6.3 Element-pairing rules

Products are tagged with **a maximum of two elements**. The first listed is primary, the second is secondary. The pair must be philosophically coherent (Agni + Vayu = energising + focusing makes sense; Akasha + Prithvi = ether + earth would not).

Never claim a product covers all five elements. That's mysticism, not Ayurveda.

## 6.4 What the Pancha system is NOT

- Not dosha (Vata/Pitta/Kapha). Different framework. We can reference dosha in long-form editorial content but the product system is element-based, not dosha-based.
- Not chakras. Don't conflate.
- Not religion. The elements are an empirical model of experience, not a spiritual claim.
- Not a personality test. The bar chart returns *current state*, not *type*. It can change visit-to-visit.

# PART 7 · VISUAL LANGUAGE (DIRECTION 3 — WARM EDITORIAL)

This is the master visual system. Every brand surface — homepage, packaging, Instagram, OOH, packaging inserts, email — uses this system unless explicitly assigned to Direction 1 (Dark Apothecary, for manifesto/about/founder essay) or Direction 2 (Bold Clinical, for product pages and quiz results).

## 7.1 The four foundational principles

Drawn from the founder's prior design principles (Balance Over Emphasis, Dense But Layered, Healthcare-Grade Trust, Narrative First).

### Principle 01 · Balance over emphasis
Hierarchy comes from relationships — typographic scale, weight contrast, spacing — not from volume. Single accent colour for action, never size escalation or colour shouting. No element shouts.

### Principle 02 · Dense, but layered
Show everything the customer needs in clearly separated strata. Strict sectioning, predictable card patterns, accordion-based progressive disclosure, ruthless typographic discipline within each stratum. Don't allow two adjacent sections to share background, padding, AND type.

### Principle 03 · Healthcare-grade trust
Every claim builds credibility, never decorates the UI. Clear provenance, clinical-study badges, named ingredient geographies, study details (sample size, duration). Take cues from Ritual.

### Principle 04 · Narrative first, commerce embedded
Customers don't buy a SKU; they buy the logic behind it. Lead category and PDP pages with the concern, root cause, mechanism, and evidence — then let the product emerge naturally. Build commerce around sourcing, doshas, studies, quizzes, ingredient deep-dives, and protocols — not separated into blogs while PDPs start with price and product images.

### Colour rules — non-negotiable

1. **Sienna is reserved for italic-serif accent text only.** Never a background, never a border, never an icon fill. The single exception: the in-house Vaidya signature mark, which can use Sienna.
2. **Saffron is the only "earned" CTA colour.** Used for the single most important action on a page. If a page has two important actions, Saffron goes to one; the other gets Ink (or transparent + Ink border) on a light surface, or Bone on Cocoa.
3. **Element full colours appear as small accents only** — element dots (8–14 px), small icons, tiny stripes. Never as a card background fill or page surface.
4. **Element washes are the surface format.** Use a 50% wash as the background of a card to signal "this is in the X element zone."
5. **Never use more than one accent colour per visual surface.** A card is either Agni-coded OR Vayu-coded — never both. The exception is the elemental bar (a 5-stripe horizontal brand stripe).
6. **No gradients except in Direction 1 (Dark Apothecary) and the dark-variant hero in Direction 3.** And those gradients are warm cocoa-to-amber, no other.
7. **Black is never #000000.** Always Ink (`#2A2118`) or Cocoa (`#2A1F18`).
8. **Pure white is rare.** Default to Butter or Cream. White is only used in Direction 2 (Bold Clinical) surfaces.

### Typefaces

**Why these typefaces:**
- **Inter** is the modern healthcare-DTC default (Ritual, Function Health, Ro, Hims, AG1 all use it or close cousins). Highly readable, no quirky personality, generous spacing.

- **IBM Plex Mono** is the caption/data voice. It reads "lab document" and "field notes" simultaneously. Uppercase + letter-spaced.

### Type rules — non-negotiable

1. **"elements" is always lowercase.** Logo, body, mid-sentence, anywhere. The only exception is when the sentence begins with it and we deliberately want emphasis — even then, prefer to restructure the sentence.
2. **Sentence case everywhere.** No Title Case. Not on H1s, not on buttons, not on category labels. The eyebrows are UPPERCASE but they're mono caps, which is a separate visual register.
3. **One italic-serif accent per headline maximum.** The pattern is: sans-serif main + ONE italic-serif word in Sienna. Never multiple italics in one headline. Never italicise the sans typeface — always switch to Cormorant.
4. **The italic accent word is the emotional payload.** It's the verb, the adjective, or the noun that the consumer takes home with them. "Sleep better, *finally*." — the takeaway is "finally," the relief. "Your morning, *examined*." — the takeaway is the careful look.
5. **No bold body text.** Emphasis in body copy is done with Cormorant italic or with Inter 500 weight at most. No `<strong>` in default styles.
6. **Numbers in IBM Plex Mono when they're data.** "Cortisol −27.9%" is mono. "Two minutes" is Inter. The rule: if it's a measurement, citation, batch number, or technical spec, it's mono.
7. **Line length: 60–72 characters for body, 30–45 for display.** No copy that runs to the full container width on desktop.
8. **No all-caps body text.** Mono caps are eyebrows and labels only — never used for paragraph-length copy.

### The italic-keyword pattern — THE signature move

This is the single most important typographic decision in the brand. It's the visual move that, once consistently used, becomes inimitable.

**Pattern:**
```
[Inter 500 sans-serif sentence] [Cormorant Garamond 400 italic word in Sienna] [optional continuation of sans sentence]
```

**Examples in use:**

> Sleep better, *finally.*

> Calm-alert *mornings.*

> Sharper focus, less *jitter.*

> Your morning, *examined.*

> The 5,000-year-old *answer* to modern stress.

> Five elements. One quiz. Your morning, *mapped.*

> The ritual you've always *deserved.*

> What founders take instead of a *fourth* coffee.

> Built for the way you *actually* live.

**Variations by surface:**
- **On Butter/Cream surfaces:** italic accent in Sienna `#C4541E`
- **On Cocoa surfaces:** italic accent in Saffron `#F0B947`
- **On element-wash surfaces:** italic accent in the corresponding element solid colour
- **In pill cards and concern statements:** the italic accent often precedes the Pancha element name in italic (e.g., "Sleep better, *finally*" + element dot + italic "*prithvi*")

**Rules:**
1. Always switch typefaces — never italicise Inter
2. Always Cormorant Garamond 400 italic for the accent
3. Always one italic word per headline maximum
4. The italic word is *meaningful*, not decorative. If you can replace it without losing the headline's emotional payload, the italic is being misused.
5. Don't italicise common verbs ("*is*", "*will*", "*has*"). Italicise the noun, the adjective, or the action verb that carries the headline.

## 7.7 Photography direction

**Light, surface, object — in that order of importance.**

### Light
- Natural light, indoor or shaded outdoor
- Time of day: 7–10 am or 4–6 pm — never harsh midday
- Soft shadows, never high contrast
- Warm white balance, no cool-blue cast
- Backlit when possible — light coming through windows, steam, glass

### Surface
- Linen, raw paper, unfinished wood, stone, ceramic
- Avoid: glass coffee tables, marble (too luxury), white seamless (too clinical)
- Warm neutrals — the colour family of Butter and Cream

### Object
- The product alone is fine. The product with a single hand is better. The product as part of a small still life (cup, leaf, journal, water glass) is the best.
- Faces are rare — reserved for founder content, Vaidya features, and ambassador testimonials
- When faces appear: 3/4 angle, eyes downcast or off-camera, never direct-to-lens
- People shot from behind or torso-only when context matters more than identity

### Composition
- Centred or rule-of-thirds, never asymmetric-creative
- Generous negative space — 50–70% of the frame
- Single subject per frame
- Steam, hands, water surface — these are the brand's atmospheric details

### Casting
- Indian. Tier-1 metro affect — relatable, not aspirational-distant
- Age range matching personas: 28–45 dominant, 22–28 for Gen Z campaigns
- Skin tones: full range, no whitewashing
- Wardrobe: linen, cotton, muted earth tones — never branded athleisure, never silk saris

### What to avoid
- No stock photography energy (smiling-at-yoga-mat genre)
- No mystic-Indian-temple imagery
- No saffron-orange wardrobe or props
- No marble, no gold rim, no luxury-aspirational cues
- No before/after shots (we're not a transformation brand)
- No tablet/screen-in-hand modernity tropes

# PART 8 · BRAND VOICE

## 8.1 Voice principles

**Quiet authority.** We know what we're talking about. We don't shout about it. We don't need to.

**Conversational but specific.** "We made this for the morning that refuses to begin" reads better than "designed for energy and focus." Specifics earn the reader's attention.

**Citations always present.** Every clinical claim is cited. Footnotes are normalised. The Vaidya signs off. The batch number is on the page.

**Literate, not academic.** A reader who finished her English degree should feel addressed. A reader who didn't should not feel excluded. Aim for the New Yorker's plainspoken precision, not the medical journal's hedging.

**Indian, not Indianised.** Don't translate Sanskrit terms unless context requires. "Vaidya" is a vaidya — not a "wellness practitioner." "Triphala" is triphala — not "three-fruit cleansing blend." But also: don't lean on Sanskrit as exotica. The reader knows what we mean.

**No mysticism.** Ayurveda is empirical. "Vayu imbalance" is acceptable — it's classical terminology. "Harness your inner fire" is not — it's wellness-influencer prose.

# PART 10 · THE DIGITAL EXPERIENCE

## 10.1 The Pancha Quiz flow (master flow)

**Step 0 — Quiz entry.** Homepage hero card "See your elemental profile" → quiz landing.

**Step 1 — Frame.** "Before we suggest anything, we'd like to understand your mornings. Seven questions. Two minutes. No email required. Our in-house vaidya reviews every answer pattern."

**Step 2–8 — Seven questions.** Each question maps to elemental signals. Examples:
- *How do your mornings usually feel?* (energy / agitation / lethargy / steady — Agni indicators)
- *When you can't sleep, what's keeping you up?* (racing mind / restlessness / body tension / nothing identifiable — Vayu + Prithvi indicators)
- *How would you describe your focus through a working day?* (sharp then crashes / scattered / steady / blanks out — Vayu + Akasha indicators)
- *What's your relationship with stress right now?* (chronic / acute / managed / not currently a problem — Jala + Prithvi)
- *How are your digestion patterns?* (sluggish / variable / strong / irregular — Agni + Prithvi)
- *What do you reach for when overwhelmed?* (coffee / scroll / sleep / nothing helps — diagnostic across)
- *What word describes how you'd like to feel by 11 am?* (alert / calm / grounded / clear / fired-up — confirms the priority element)

**Step 9 — Calculation.** Backend assigns weighted points to each element based on response combinations. Returns a percentage breakdown summing to 100%.

**Step 10 — Result.** Elemental profile bar chart + a 2-sentence interpretation co-signed by the Vaidya. Example:

> *"You are running high in Vayu, low in Prithvi. Translation: too much mental movement, not enough grounding. A common pattern in 27- to 38-year-olds in metro tech roles. Reviewed by Dr. Anjali Iyer, BAMS · in-house vaidya."*

**Step 11 — The 14-day ritual recommendation.** 2–3 products from the catalogue, each tagged to the element it balances. Coffee + Ashwagandha is the most common output for the Bengaluru Burnout.

**Step 12 — AI Vaidya invitation.** "Want to ask follow-up questions? Meet the AI Vaidya, trained on classical texts and our clinical content. Co-signed by Dr. Iyer."

**Step 13 — Conversion.** Bundle CTA "Start the 14-day ritual" (Saffron, single button). Subscription option below at -15%.

## 10.2 The AI Vaidya

## 12.1 Decision-making framework

When proposing or building anything, Claude should evaluate against these questions in order:

1. **Does this serve the Bengaluru Burnout?** (primary persona — 50% weight)
2. **Does this respect the Pancha system?** (every system-level decision)
3. **Does this hold the Direction 3 visual language strictly?** (every UI/visual decision)

5. **Is there a citation or batch reference for any clinical claim?** (every copy decision)
6. **Does the founder's voice or the Vaidya's voice get diluted by what I'm proposing?** (every long-form copy)

If three of six come back "no," propose an alternative. Don't build it.

- **Be a senior collaborator, not an assistant.** Argue. Push back. Propose. Defend with evidence.
